Boat Keeper

Your boat needs regular attention whether you're aboard or not. The Boat Keeper service keeps your vessel clean, dry, operational, and ready to go — on a schedule that fits how often you actually use it.

Boats that sit untended develop problems quietly. Bilge pumps fail, batteries drain, lines chafe, mildew spreads through the cabin, and wood finishes break down under UV and salt exposure. Each of those problems is cheap to catch early and expensive to fix late. Boat Keeper is structured around preventing that gap.

What Each Visit Covers

  • Exterior wash-down — hull sides, decks, non-skid, glass and stainless
  • Bilge check — inspect pump operation, clear debris, note water intrusion
  • Battery voltage check — house and start banks, shore power connections
  • Dock line and fender inspection — chafe points, cleat hardware, adjustment for tide and weather
  • Thru-hull and seacock spot check — visual inspection, exercise valves on schedule
  • Engine run cycle — run to operating temperature, check cooling water flow, note alarms or leaks
  • Cabin ventilation — open hatches, check for mildew, monitor dehumidifier if installed
  • Woodwork condition assessment — teak decks, brightwork, interior trim; note anything starting to fail before it needs a full restoration
  • Photo report — timestamped images of any findings, emailed after each visit

Every visit follows the same checklist. Nothing gets skipped because it "looked fine last time." The report tells you what was checked, what was found, and what — if anything — needs attention before the next visit.

Visit Schedules

Choose a frequency that matches how much you use the boat. Owners who are aboard weekly usually need less coverage; boats that sit for weeks or months between use need more.

Essential
Monthly

For boats used regularly. Catches slow-developing problems — battery drain, line wear, early mildew — before they compound.

Standard
Bi-weekly

For boats used a few times a month. Keeps systems exercised, hull clean enough to prevent buildup, and wood monitored through weather changes.

Full Care
Weekly

For boats that need to be ready at all times, or that sit in high-growth or high-UV locations. Includes more thorough cleaning and tighter monitoring cycles.

Wood Care Integration

Because we already do the wood restoration work, the Boat Keeper visits double as an early-warning system for your teak and brightwork. We know what failing varnish, drying teak, and UV damage look like at stage one — not stage five. If a touch-up or a re-oil can prevent a full strip-and-refinish, we'll flag it in the report and quote it before it gets worse.

Clients on a Boat Keeper plan get priority scheduling for any restoration or varnish work that comes up.

What Makes This Different

Most boat-care companies send a crew with a hose. We send someone who knows marine wood, mechanical systems, and what actually matters below the waterline. Every visit is performed by the same person who would do your restoration work — not a subcontracted cleaning crew.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size boats do you service?

Sailboats and powerboats from roughly 25 to 65 feet. Larger vessels are case-by-case — reach out and we'll discuss scope and pricing.

Can I add or pause visits seasonally?

Yes. Many owners increase frequency before a trip or during winter storage conditions, and scale back during active-use months. We adjust month to month with 48 hours' notice.

What if you find something that needs repair?

We document it in the photo report with a description and a rough cost estimate. You decide whether to authorize the work. We never perform unquoted repairs during a Keeper visit.

Is this a contract?

Month-to-month. No long-term commitment. Cancel or pause any time with 48 hours' notice before the next scheduled visit.

Do you work on boats in dry storage?

Yes — the checklist adapts. No hull wash or thru-hull checks on a hard-stand boat, but battery, cabin, cover, and wood monitoring all still apply.
